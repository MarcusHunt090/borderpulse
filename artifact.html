    Data & methods

    How the score works

    Labor (40%): El Paso unemployment, change over 6 months; total payroll jobs, growth from a year earlier.
    Trade & currency (35%): U.S. imports from and exports to Mexico, growth from a year earlier; peso/dollar, 6-month move.
    Housing (25%): active home listings and median days on market, change from a year earlier. A for-sale market measure, not household finances.
    No look-ahead: every z-score uses only history up to its own month, so the back-test is honest.

    Reading the levels: 50 is a typical month for El Paso. Below 35 Calm · 35–50 Near normal · 50–65 Above normal · 65 and up High (the alert zone). The score is an index, not a probability: 40 does not mean a 40% chance of anything.

    Formulas

    For each indicator, the raw series becomes a pressure signal s(t) (6-month difference or percent change, or 12-month change; see the dictionary), standardized as z(t) = clip((s(t) − mean(s(1..t))) / sd(s(1..t)), ±3) using only history through t (minimum 36 observations; 24 for the short housing series). Layer scores average their indicators’ z; the overall z is the weighted layer average (weights renormalized when a layer is absent); the displayed score is 100 / (1 + e^(−1.1·z)). The back-test rebuilds every z with publication delays (labor and housing t−1, trade t−2, peso t−0) so each month uses only information available then. Level decomposition and month-over-month attribution are exact in z and pro-rata in points; both ship in borderpulse_output.json.
